Abstract

Monocular Gaussian SLAM estimates camera motion and builds photorealistic 3D maps from RGB video, but dense tracking and Gaussian mapping demand substantial GPU compute and memory. Replacing dense tracking with sparse tracking reduces the tracking burden, but two challenges remain when connecting a loop-closing sparse tracker to dense Gaussian mapping. First, sparse tracking provides insufficient geometric priors for dense Gaussian initialization, while existing densification either retains spatial distortions and cross-frame fluctuations after global alignment or reintroduces dense computation through windowed multiview estimation. Second, loop closure revises previously published keyframes, making the Gaussian map inconsistent with the final trajectory if these states are treated as immutable.

To address these challenges, we present STREAM-SLAM, which treats tracking outputs as a stream of revisable sparse geometric states. Sparse-conditioned depth completion anchors dense geometry to tracked sparse depths, and multiview support filtering rejects unsupported Gaussian initialization. Retrospective correction uses source-keyframe ownership to propagate subsequent Sim(3), pose, and depth changes to the corresponding Gaussians. A bounded correction-aware asynchronous protocol decouples tracking and mapping while ensuring complete delivery of keyframes and corrections.

Experiments on Replica, EuRoC, TUM RGB-D, and Waymo demonstrate competitive trajectory accuracy and strong reconstruction quality with high processing efficiency and low peak GPU memory.
